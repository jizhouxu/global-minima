A useful writing brief tells my AI collaborator what I am trying to say, who I
want to reach, and where I am uncertain. That gives us something to work on
together. “Make this better” leaves too many of the important choices
unspecified.

The working arrangement for this site is straightforward. The core views and
direction are mine. AI can help with research, structure, arguments, drafts,
and editing. It can propose a different conclusion and explain why. I decide
whether that conclusion becomes mine, and I am responsible for the work I
publish.

The process below is how I want that arrangement to operate. It applies to an
essay, a technical explanation, or a short note. The amount of process should
fit the task.

First, give the piece a brief.

For a substantial essay, I want a few lines that identify the reader, my
central point, the material supporting it, and what I hope the reader will
understand afterward. If I am exploring a question, the brief should say so.
A provisional idea deserves room to develop.

For example, a brief for this piece could read:

> Write for a thoughtful reader who is curious about using AI but worried about
> losing ownership of the work. Explain a process they can try. My core view is
> that I own the direction and published claims, while AI can help develop and
> test their expression. Use illustrative examples and do not invent personal
> experiences or measured improvements.

This is enough direction to begin. An assistant can infer ordinary choices
about length and structure from the assignment. It should surface a missing
decision when that decision would materially change the argument.

Next, separate what we believe from what we can support.

A draft can contain an observation, an inference, a preference, and a prediction
in the same paragraph. They need different treatment. A preference can be stated
as mine. An outside factual claim needs evidence. An inference needs a visible
connection to that evidence. A prediction needs the assumptions that could make
it fail.

For research, I want a source attached to the particular claim it supports. We
should open it, check the relevant passage, and preserve its limits. A search
result or a model's remembered quotation gives us somewhere to look. It does
not complete the check.

Consider an illustrative claim: “AI makes writing faster.” Before treating
that as a general result, we would need evidence about the task, the writers,
and what counted as finished. For this essay, my actual point is narrower:
I want AI to help me improve a draft, and I will judge the help by what survives
careful revision. There is no need to decorate that intention with a performance
claim.

Then make something we can examine.

When the argument is clear, an outline can establish the order. When it is
still forming, one useful paragraph may be the right place to start. I can
supply notes, a question, or rough prose; AI can draft from that material.
The instruction should make clear which parts are settled and which remain
open.

I do not need every sentence to sound finished at this stage. I need enough
substance to see where the argument goes. A working draft may contain an
explicit note that a claim needs verification. That note must be resolved
before publication.

Once there is a draft, the most useful next request is often a challenge:

> Restate my central claim. Find the strongest relevant objection, the weakest
> inference, and any step a reader would have to supply. Explain what evidence
> or narrower wording would help. Keep proposed changes to my position separate
> from edits that clarify it.

For an essay about AI and authorship, a serious objection is that a writer could
approve fluent prose without understanding it. The protocol needs an answer to
that concern. Reading the final draft is necessary; being able to explain the
argument and its support is a stronger test of ownership.

I want AI to press on that gap. Agreement alone would give me little reason to
trust the collaboration.

After the argument holds together, inspect its order.

Ask the assistant to label each paragraph's job in a few words. Read those
labels in sequence. They reveal whether an example arrives before the idea it
illuminates, whether an objection is answered, and whether several paragraphs
repeat the same point. Move or cut a section before spending time perfecting
its sentences.

The reader's needs determine the sequence. Instructions should get them to the
next action. A technical explanation should introduce the necessary terms and
show an example before adding complexity. An exploratory essay can take a
longer route if the reader understands what question makes the journey
worthwhile.

Only then spend sustained attention on the language.

I want proposed edits to show their effect on meaning. Here is a small example,
written for illustration:

> Original: “AI can help clarify an argument when the writer has enough
> understanding to evaluate the revision.”
>
> Careless cut: “AI clarifies arguments.”
>
> Better cut: “AI can clarify an argument if the writer can judge the revision.”

The shortest version changes the claim. It removes both the uncertainty and
the condition. A useful editor can explain why the slightly longer sentence
deserves its space.

This pass also checks rhythm and voice. Short sentences can give a difficult
idea room. A longer sentence can show how its parts belong together. I want
specific observations and words I would use in conversation with an interested
reader. Personal texture has to come from material I actually supply.

Finally, read the piece as something another person will encounter.

The title makes a promise. The opening should establish it, the body should
develop it, and the ending should finish the work. Check the factual claims,
quotations, links, and any technical examples on which the explanation depends.
Remove unresolved notes. Revisit any sentence that acquired more certainty
during editing than the evidence supports.

My final questions are practical: Can I explain this? Do I mean it? Does the
reader have what they need? Where AI materially helped, I can describe that
help plainly. Its involvement does not relieve me of those questions.
